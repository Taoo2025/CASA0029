Chapter 01 · Context

Accessibility is fragile.

Transport for London publishes the PTAL index — a static measure of how well a place is served by public transport. But PTAL describes a perfect world: every line running, every stop reachable.

Real disruptions — strikes, signal failures, route cancellations — happen weekly. PTAL says nothing about them. Yet the same household can wake up in PTAL 6 and find themselves stranded in PTAL 2 by lunchtime.

We need a measure that asks: after a route is cut, how much accessibility actually remains?

The static vs. the dynamic view

PTAL gives you the snapshot; LTRS gives you the stress test.
PTAL
Static. Frequencies × walking time. Tells you what an area looks like on a normal Wednesday.
LTRS
Local Transport Resilience Score. Asks: when route X dies, what fraction of accessibility survives?
Chapter 02 · Method

How we score resilience.

Four data layers feed a single live equation. Cancel a route and every downstream metric — grid AI, LSOA reachability, LTRS — recomputes in the browser.

STEP 01

Build the network

OSM walking graph + every TfL stop (bus / tube / DLR / Overground / Elizabeth / National Rail), each tagged with the routes that serve it.

STEP 02

Compute baseline AI

For each 100 m grid cell, run Dijkstra walking times to nearby stops, weight by line frequencies — classic PTAL-style Accessibility Index.

STEP 03

Simulate cancellations

Drop a route from the graph. A stop survives only if ≥ 1 of its remaining routes is still active. Recompute AI.

STEP 04

Derive LTRS

LTRS = disrupted AI / baseline AI. ≥ 80 % = resilient. < 50 % = vulnerable. Map it, compare it, drill into it.

Chapter 06 · What we learned

Three things you can't see in PTAL.

FINDING 01

Vulnerability ≠ centrality.

The most "damaging" cancellations aren't always Tube lines. Several outer-London bus routes — where alternatives are sparse — strip access for more LSOAs than a Zone 1 Tube line ever would.

FINDING 02

Resilience is granular.

Two adjacent 100 m grid cells can score 100 % and 35 % LTRS for the same disruption. Aggregating to LSOA hides the cliff edges where vulnerability lives.

FINDING 03

The Hub Paradox.

Areas with high PTAL often have abundant alternatives, so they absorb cancellations gracefully. Medium-PTAL edges of the city — single-route dependents — pay the steepest resilience cost.
