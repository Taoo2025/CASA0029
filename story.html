Chapter 01 · Background

A snapshot is not a stress test.

Transport for London publishes the Public Transport Accessibility Level (PTAL) — a single score that tells you how reachable a neighbourhood is on a normal day, when every line is running and every stop is open.

But disruption is the rule, not the exception. A signal failure, a closure or a cancellation can pull whole streets out of the network in minutes. PTAL has nothing to say about that.

This project adds the missing layer: after a route is cut, how much access is left, and where? The result is a resilience score for every 100 m square in Greater London.

Two complementary lenses

PTAL describes the world. Resilience describes what happens when it breaks.
Baseline
How easy is it to reach public transport from this place today, with everything running?
Resilience
How much of that access survives when a chosen route is removed from the network?
Chapter 02 · Key metrics

The four numbers worth knowing.

Each formula below is written in plain mathematics — no programming, just a recipe. Read the formula on the left, the human translation on the right.

01

Accessibility Index (AI)

AI  =  Σ ( weight × proximity )
For each spot we add up every nearby stop, weighing each by how close it is and how good its service is. A high AI means many stops, many lines, all within walking distance. This is the baseline — life on a normal day.
02

Disrupted Accessibility

AIafter  =  AIbefore  −  (loss from cancelled routes)
When a route is cancelled, every cell that depended on it loses some of its score. We pre-compute that loss for all 498 routes, so the website can subtract it instantly as you click.
03

Resilience Score (LTRS)

LTRS  =  AIafter ÷ AIbefore
The fraction of access that remains. A score close to 1 means the neighbourhood barely notices. Below 0.3 means a critical loss — the route was nearly the only thing serving it.
04

Walking delay

delay  =  new walking time  −  old walking time
The extra minutes residents must walk to reach the next-best stop after the cancellation. We cap "good access" at 12 minutes — the standard threshold in transport planning.
Chapter 03 · How to use the map

Four moves to read the city.

The interactive map opens in a new tab. Here is the workflow we suggest — each step takes a single click.

1

Open the analysis panel

The map starts clean. Click Analysis at the bottom of the screen to slide in the controls — search routes, browse boroughs, pick scenarios.

2

Read the baseline

Every 100 m cell is shaded by its baseline AI. Sage means well-served, terracotta means a transport gap. Click any cell to see which routes feed it.

3

Cancel a route

Pick any route from the right panel and tick Cancel. The disruption layer appears: red where access is lost, untouched where alternatives exist.

4

Compare side by side

Drag the divider to split the view. Baseline on the left, disrupted on the right. The Selected Grid card on the panel shows exact AI before / after / LTRS.
