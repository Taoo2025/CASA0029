Chapter 01 · Context

PTAL tells you the snapshot.
It can't tell you the stress test.

Transport for London publishes the Public Transport Accessibility Level (PTAL) — a static index combining stop frequencies and walking times. It maps London on a normal Wednesday, every line running, every stop reachable.

Real disruptions happen weekly. A signal failure, an industrial action, a single route cancellation can drop a household from PTAL 6 at breakfast to PTAL 2 at lunch. PTAL has nothing to say about it.

This project adds the missing layer: after a route is cut, how much accessibility actually remains — and where?

Static index vs. dynamic resilience

Two complementary lenses on the same network.
PTAL · Baseline
Frequency-weighted walking access to nearby stops at full service.
AI = Σ (rank_w · decay · 10)
LTRS · Resilience
Fraction of AI surviving when a chosen route is removed from the graph.
LTRS = AI_dis / AI_base
Chapter 02 · Method

Four data layers, one live equation.

Cancel a route in the browser and every downstream metric — grid AI, LSOA reachability, LTRS classification, the disruption colour map — recomputes against pre-built impact tables in < 100 ms.

STEP 01 · INGEST

Build the unified network

Merge TfL NAPTAN bus, Tube, DLR, Elizabeth Line, Overground and Tramlink stop datasets into 27,553 de-duplicated stops, each tagged with the routes that serve it via KDTree spatial joins.

STEP 02 · GRAPH

OSM walking Dijkstra

Filter Greater London OSM roads to walkable classes (footway, residential, tertiary…), reproject to EPSG:27700, snap stops & grid centroids, run Dijkstra to compute true walking times — not Euclidean fudge.

STEP 03 · BASELINE AI

PTAL-style decay model

For each 100 m cell: query stops within 1200 m, apply impedance max(0, 1 − d/c) with 640 m bus / 960 m rail catchment, weight by route rank (1.0 / 0.5), accumulate per-route contributions.

STEP 04 · IMPACT MATRIX

Pre-compute every cancellation

Aggregate per-route AI loss across all 159,451 grids into a sparse route × grid impact table. Cancel any combination in the UI; LTRS becomes an arithmetic lookup, not a re-simulation.

Chapter 04 · Definitions

The metrics, precisely.

Every number in the live app maps to one of these definitions.

Baseline AI
Σ rank_w · max(0, 1 − d/c) · 10
PTAL-style accessibility index per 100 m cell. Sum over reachable stops × routes, weighted by walking distance and rank within mode.
Disrupted AI
AI_base − Σ AI_loss(r) , r ∈ cancelled
Same cell after removing every contribution from the cancelled routes — drawn directly from the pre-computed impact table.
LTRS
AI_dis / AI_base ∈ [0, 1]
Local Transport Resilience Score. ≥ 0.8 resilient, 0.6–0.8 moderate, 0.3–0.6 high impact, < 0.3 critical.
Mean delay (min)
avg Δ walk-min to nearest stop
How much further you must walk after the cancellation, averaged across LSOAs where the route was previously the closest option.
Lost 12-min access
count: base ≤ 12 ∧ disrupted > 12
The hard cliff: LSOAs that fall off the standard 12-minute walking-access threshold purely because of this route's removal.
Retention ratio
(affected − lost) / affected
Of the LSOAs whose nearest stop was on this route, what fraction still meet the 12-minute threshold via an alternative service.

Chapter 08 · What we learned

Three things PTAL alone won't show you.

FINDING 01

Vulnerability ≠ centrality.

The most damaging cancellations aren't always Tube lines. Several outer-London bus routes — where a cell has no second-best alternative within 1.2 km — strip 12-minute access from more LSOAs than any Zone 1 Tube line.

routes 191, 99, H12 · 110+ LSOAs each
FINDING 02

Resilience is granular.

Two adjacent 100 m grid cells can score 100 % and 35 % LTRS for the same disruption. Aggregating to LSOA averages out the cliff edges where vulnerability actually lives — the 100 m grid is essential.

122,315 grids with non-zero baseline AI
FINDING 03

The Hub Paradox.

Inner London absorbs cancellations gracefully — multiple overlapping routes cushion every cell. The pain concentrates in medium-PTAL outer zones (Croydon, Sutton, Barking, Newham) where households depend on a single line.

96.7 % LSOAs reach a stop in ≤ 12 min at baseline
